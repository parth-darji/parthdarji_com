How to read an EOB (Explanation of Benefits)

By Parth Darji · Updated November 23, 2026

In shortAn EOB comes from your insurer after a claim. It is not a bill. It shows what the provider charged, what insurance paid, and what you owe ("patient responsibility"). For your HSA, keep the EOB together with the provider's bill or receipt and proof that you paid.

What's on an EOB

LineWhat it means
Provider and date of serviceWho treated you and when
Amount billedThe provider's full price
Allowed amount / network discountThe price your plan agreed with the provider; the difference is usually written off
Plan paidWhat insurance paid
Deductible, copay, coinsuranceYour share, split by type
Not coveredCharges the plan didn't pay, with a reason code
Patient responsibilityThe total you may owe the provider

EOB vs bill

The provider's bill tells you what to pay; the EOB tells you what you should owe under your plan. Before paying, check that the bill matches the patient responsibility on the EOB. If it doesn't, call the provider or your insurer first.

Using an EOB for your HSA

  • You can reimburse yourself for what you actually paid, up to the patient responsibility, not the amount billed.
  • The EOB shows the service, date and your share. Add the provider's receipt or a card or bank statement line as proof of payment.
  • Many EOBs arrive as PDFs in your insurer's app. Save them together with the receipt so you can find both years later.

Questions

Is an EOB a bill?

No. It's a statement from your insurer. The provider sends the bill; the EOB shows what you should owe under your plan.

Is an EOB enough proof for an HSA withdrawal?

It shows the service, date and your share. Keep proof that you paid as well, such as the provider's receipt or a card statement.

What does patient responsibility mean on an EOB?

The total you may owe the provider after insurance: your deductible, copay, coinsurance and any non-covered charges.

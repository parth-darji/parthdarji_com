Does HSA money roll over?

By Parth Darji · Updated November 23, 2026

In shortYes. Unlike an FSA, your HSA never expires: the money stays yours every year, even if you change jobs or health plans. You can also move it to a different HSA provider.

Year to year

There's no "use it or lose it." Unused money stays in the account and can keep growing. That's the key difference from an FSA: HSA vs FSA.

Moving to another HSA provider

  • Trustee-to-trustee transfer (easiest). The new provider pulls the money from the old one. No limit on how often, nothing to report as income.
  • 60-day rollover. You take the money out and deposit it in the new HSA within 60 days. Allowed once every 12 months. Miss the deadline and it may be taxed and penalized.

Common reasons to move: lower fees, better investment options, or leaving an employer's HSA after a job change. Your money stays yours either way.

IRA to HSA: once in a lifetime

You can move money from a traditional or Roth IRA into your HSA once in your life, up to that year's contribution limit. It counts toward the limit, and you must stay HSA-eligible for a 12-month testing period afterwards. Check IRS Publication 969 before doing it.

HSA to IRA: not allowed

There's no tax-free way to move HSA money into an IRA or 401(k). It would count as a non-medical withdrawal. After 65, though, non-medical HSA withdrawals are taxed like a traditional IRA with no penalty. See the withdrawal calculator.

Your receipts move with you

Receipts for expenses from your old HSA years can still be reimbursed from the new account, as long as you've had an HSA without a gap. Keep them somewhere that isn't tied to an employer.

Questions

Does HSA money roll over every year?

Yes. HSA money never expires and rolls over every year, unlike most FSAs.

Can I roll my HSA into an IRA?

No. Moving HSA money to an IRA counts as a non-medical withdrawal. After 65 it's taxed as income with no penalty.

Can I roll an IRA into an HSA?

Once in your lifetime, up to that year's HSA contribution limit, if you stay HSA-eligible for 12 months afterwards.

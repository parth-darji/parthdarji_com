The HSA triple tax advantage, explained

By Parth Darji · Updated November 23, 2026

In shortAn HSA is the only US account that's tax-free three times: money goes in before tax, grows without tax, and comes out tax-free for medical expenses. The third break is the one people waste, because they don't keep receipts.

The three tax breaks

  1. Tax-free in. Contributions are deducted from your income. Through payroll, they also skip Social Security and Medicare tax. Calculate your savings.
  2. Tax-free growth. Interest and investment gains aren't taxed while they stay in the account.
  3. Tax-free out. Withdrawals for qualified medical expenses are never taxed, even decades later.

An example

Say you put $1,000 into your HSA through payroll at a 22% federal bracket and 5% state tax. You skip about $347 of tax up front. If it's invested and grows to $3,000 over 20 years, you take the full $3,000 out tax-free for medical bills. In a regular brokerage account, you'd have started with about $653 and paid tax on the gains too.

Growth isn't guaranteed: investments can lose value. Try your own numbers in the growth calculator.

How people get the most out of it

Pay current medical bills from regular savings, leave the HSA invested, keep every receipt, and reimburse yourself years later. That's the shoebox strategy. It only works if you can prove each expense, so keep the receipts.

States that don't follow it

California and New Jersey tax HSAs at the state level, so you get the federal breaks but not the state ones. Check your state's rules.

Questions

What is the HSA triple tax advantage?

HSA contributions are tax-deductible, growth inside the account isn't taxed, and withdrawals for qualified medical expenses are tax-free.

Is an HSA better than a 401(k)?

For medical costs, an HSA is more tax-efficient because qualified withdrawals are never taxed. Many people get any 401(k) employer match first, then fund the HSA. It depends on your situation.

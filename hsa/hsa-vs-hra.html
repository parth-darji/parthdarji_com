HSA vs HRA: what's the difference?

By Parth Darji · Updated November 23, 2026

In shortAn HSA is your own account: you keep it forever, you and your employer can add money, and you can invest it. An HRA belongs to your employer: only the employer funds it, and you usually lose it when you leave.

Can you have both?

Only if the HRA is HSA-compatible, such as a limited-purpose HRA (dental and vision only) or a post-deductible HRA. A general HRA that pays medical bills before your deductible makes you ineligible to contribute to an HSA. Ask your benefits team which type you have. More: HSA-eligible health plans.

Which is better?

If you can choose, an HSA usually gives you more: ownership, tax-free growth and no deadline to reimburse yourself. But free employer HRA money is still free money. Compare plans with the HSA vs PPO calculator.

Questions

What is the difference between an HSA and an HRA?

You own an HSA, can add your own money, invest it and keep it forever. An HRA is owned and funded only by your employer and usually ends when you leave.

Can I have an HSA and an HRA at the same time?

Only if the HRA is HSA-compatible, such as a limited-purpose (dental and vision) or post-deductible HRA.

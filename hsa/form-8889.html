Form 8889, explained in plain English

By Parth Darji · Updated November 22, 2026

In shortForm 8889 is the HSA page of your tax return. It reports money that went into your HSA, money that came out, and whether what came out was for qualified medical expenses. If you or your employer put money in, or you took money out, you generally need it.

Who has to file it

You generally file Form 8889 with your Form 1040 if, during the year, you or anyone else (like your employer) contributed to your HSA, you took money out of it, or you have to report HSA amounts as income. Married couples who each have an HSA each file their own.

The three parts of the form

  1. Contributions and deduction. Your coverage type (self-only or family), what was put in, and how much you can deduct. Money put in through payroll is already left out of your taxable wages.
  2. Distributions. The total you took out (from Form 1099-SA), how much of it paid for qualified medical expenses, and any taxable amount plus the 20% additional tax if it applies.
  3. Income for not staying eligible. Only for special cases, such as using the "last-month rule" and then not keeping a qualifying health plan for the following year.

Reimbursing yourself for older receipts

With the shoebox strategy, the expense and the withdrawal can be years apart. In the year you take the money out, you report the withdrawal, and you report the qualified medical expenses it paid for, even if those expenses happened in earlier years, as long as they came after your HSA was opened and weren't paid or deducted another way. Keep the receipts with your tax records. How to reimburse yourself.

Common mistakes

  • Forgetting the form in a year you took money out, even if it was all for medical bills.
  • Counting employer contributions again as a deduction.
  • Putting in more than the yearly limit (2026: $4,400 self-only, $8,750 family, plus $1,000 if 55 or older).
  • Withdrawing more than your qualified expenses without realizing the extra is taxable.

Questions

Do I need to file Form 8889 if I didn't use my HSA?

Usually yes if you or your employer contributed to it during the year. If there were no contributions and no withdrawals, you generally don't.

Where do I find my HSA withdrawals for Form 8889?

On Form 1099-SA from your HSA provider, which shows the total distributions for the year.

Can I report expenses from earlier years on Form 8889?

Yes, for the withdrawal that paid for them, if they happened after your HSA was opened and weren't paid by insurance or deducted.

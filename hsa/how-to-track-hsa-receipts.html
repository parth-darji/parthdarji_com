How to keep track of HSA receipts: 5 ways compared

By Parth Darji · Updated November 23, 2026

Disclosure: I'm Parth, the developer of ShoeboxHSA, one of the options below. I've tried to be fair: several free options work well, and the best one is the one you'll actually keep using for years.

In shortAny system works if it keeps a readable copy of each itemized receipt, records whether you've reimbursed it, and survives a lost phone or computer. Free options (a cloud folder plus a spreadsheet) are fine if you're disciplined; an app mainly saves time and reduces mistakes.

What any system needs to do

  • Keep a readable copy of each itemized receipt or EOB (paper fades). What a receipt must show.
  • Record the date, provider, amount and patient.
  • Track which receipts you've already reimbursed, and when, so you never claim one twice.
  • Survive 10 to 30 years: a lost phone, a new computer, a closed account.

1. A paper folder

The original "shoebox". Free and simple, and fine for a few receipts a year. The problems show up later: many receipts are printed on thermal paper that fades within a few years, and nothing tells you which ones you've already reimbursed. If you go this way, photocopy or photograph each receipt and keep a written list.

2. A cloud folder plus a spreadsheet

The most popular free method. Scan or photograph each receipt into a folder (iCloud Drive, Google Drive, Dropbox), name the file with the date and provider, and keep a spreadsheet with date, provider, amount, patient, and a "reimbursed on" column. It's free, portable and easy to back up. The downside is discipline: every receipt means renaming a file and typing a row, and the two can drift apart over the years.

3. Your HSA provider's receipt upload

Many HSA providers let you upload receipts or attach them when you request a reimbursement. It's convenient and usually free. Check two things: whether you can export everything easily, and what happens to your documents if you move your HSA to another provider later. Keep your own copy either way.

4. General receipt and expense apps

Expense and receipt-scanning apps are good at capturing receipts, mostly for business expenses and bookkeeping. They usually don't track HSA-specific things like whether an expense came after your HSA was opened, or which receipts you've paid yourself back for. Some are subscriptions or upload everything to their servers, which matters for medical records.

5. ShoeboxHSA

I built it specifically for the shoebox strategy. You snap a receipt and the iPhone reads the amount, provider and date for you to check; the app shows what you haven't reimbursed yet, lets you pay yourself back for several receipts at once with the real withdrawal date, and creates an audit-ready PDF. There's no account and no server: receipts stay on your iPhone, with an optional weekly backup to a folder you choose.

Honest downsides: it's iPhone-only, the free plan covers 15 receipts (Pro is yearly or a one-time purchase), and because everything stays on your phone, you should turn on the backup so a lost phone doesn't mean lost receipts.

Which should you pick?

  • A few receipts a year and you like spreadsheets: cloud folder + spreadsheet is enough.
  • You mostly reimburse right away: your HSA provider's upload is the simplest.
  • You're doing the shoebox strategy for decades and want it quick: an app built for it saves the typing and tracks what's reimbursed.

Whatever you choose, keep a second copy somewhere else. Already lost a receipt?

Questions

What is the best way to keep track of HSA receipts?

Any method that keeps a readable copy of each itemized receipt, records whether you've reimbursed it, and is backed up. A cloud folder with a spreadsheet works for free; apps mainly save time.

Can I just keep HSA receipts in a folder?

Yes, but photograph or scan them, since thermal paper fades, and keep a list of which receipts you've reimbursed.

Does my HSA provider keep my receipts?

Many providers let you upload receipts, but keep your own copy too, especially if you might change HSA providers.

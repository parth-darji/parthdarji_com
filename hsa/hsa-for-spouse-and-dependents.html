Can I use my HSA for my spouse or kids?

By Parth Darji · Updated November 23, 2026

In shortYes. Your HSA can pay qualified medical expenses for you, your spouse and your tax dependents, even if they're on a different health plan or none at all. The big trap: an adult child on your health insurance until 26 isn't automatically someone your HSA can pay for. It depends on whether they're your tax dependent.

Who your HSA can pay for

  • You.
  • Your spouse. This works even if your spouse has their own job-based plan, isn't HSA-eligible, or has no coverage.
  • Your tax dependents. Usually children under 19, children under 24 who are full-time students, and children or relatives you support who meet the IRS dependent rules. IRS Publication 969 has the full definition.

What matters is who the expense is for, not whose health plan covers them. The receipt should show the patient's name or you should note it, so you can show later that the expense was for someone who counts.

The adult child trap (ages 19 to 26)

Health insurance lets children stay on a parent's plan until 26. HSA rules follow the tax code instead. If your 23-year-old isn't a full-time student and isn't your tax dependent, your HSA generally can't pay their medical bills tax-free, even though your insurance covers them.

The flip side: an adult child who is covered by your HSA-eligible family plan and isn't anyone's tax dependent may be able to open their own HSA and contribute to it. Check Publication 969 or ask a tax professional for your situation.

How much you can put in

If your HSA-eligible plan covers anyone besides you, the family limit applies: $8,750 for 2026 (self-only is $4,400), plus $1,000 more if you're 55 or older. Married couples share one family limit between their HSAs. More: HSA contribution limits.

Keeping receipts for family expenses

  • Write down who each receipt is for: you, your spouse, or which child.
  • Keep the same proof you'd keep for yourself: provider, date, what it was, what you paid. See which receipts to keep.
  • You can reimburse yourself years later, as long as your HSA was open when the expense happened. See the shoebox strategy.

Questions

Can I use my HSA for my spouse?

Yes. Your HSA can pay qualified medical expenses for your spouse, even if your spouse has a different health plan or isn't HSA-eligible.

Can I use my HSA for my child?

Yes, if the child is your tax dependent, which usually means under 19, or under 24 and a full-time student. A child on your health plan until 26 doesn't automatically count.

Can I use my HSA for my parents?

Only if a parent is your tax dependent under IRS rules. Otherwise, their expenses aren't qualified for your HSA.

Your HSA after 65

By Parth Darji · Updated November 23, 2026

In shortAt 65 the 20% penalty on non-medical withdrawals goes away; you pay only income tax, like a traditional IRA. Medical withdrawals, including many Medicare premiums and old saved receipts, stay tax-free. Once you enroll in Medicare, you can't put new money in.

What changes at 65

  • No more penalty. Before 65, a withdrawal that isn't for qualified medical expenses is taxed as income plus a 20% additional tax. From 65 on, it's only taxed as income.
  • Medical withdrawals are still tax-free, so it's still worth using the account for health costs first.
  • More premiums count as medical expenses. You can use HSA money tax-free for Medicare Part B and Part D premiums, Medicare Advantage premiums, and premiums for employer retiree health coverage. Medicare supplement (Medigap) premiums don't qualify.
  • Long-term care insurance premiums qualify up to a yearly limit that depends on your age.

When contributions must stop

You can't contribute to an HSA for any month you're enrolled in Medicare, including only Part A. Two details catch people out:

  • If you start Social Security benefits at 65 or later, you're usually enrolled in Medicare Part A automatically.
  • If you sign up for Medicare after 65, Part A coverage can start up to six months earlier, so many people stop HSA contributions about six months before they apply.

You can keep spending from the HSA after Medicare starts; only new contributions stop.

Your old receipts still count

Receipts you saved years ago, for expenses after your HSA was opened, can still be reimbursed tax-free after 65. That's the payoff of the shoebox strategy: a tax-free withdrawal instead of a taxable one. How to reimburse yourself.

What happens to an HSA when you die

  • Spouse as beneficiary: the HSA becomes your spouse's own HSA.
  • Anyone else: the account stops being an HSA and its value is generally taxable income to that person in that year.

Keep your beneficiary up to date with your HSA provider.

Questions

Can I use my HSA for Medicare premiums?

Yes for Medicare Part B, Part D and Medicare Advantage premiums once you're 65 or older. Medigap (Medicare supplement) premiums don't qualify.

Can I contribute to an HSA after 65?

Only if you're not enrolled in any part of Medicare. Once Medicare coverage starts, contributions must stop.

What is the penalty for HSA withdrawals after 65?

There is no 20% penalty after 65. Withdrawals not used for qualified medical expenses are taxed as ordinary income.

HSA vs FSA: what's the difference?

By Parth Darji · Updated November 22, 2026

In shortAn HSA is your own account: the money rolls over every year, can be invested, and stays with you when you change jobs, but you need a qualifying high-deductible health plan. An FSA comes from your employer, works with most health plans, and is mostly use-it-or-lose-it each year.

Which one is better?

It depends less on "better" and more on your health plan. If you're on a high-deductible plan that qualifies, an HSA is usually more flexible: the money is yours, it can grow, and there's no rush to spend it. If you're not on such a plan, an FSA is often the only option and still saves taxes on expenses you know are coming, like glasses or braces.

FSAs have one advantage: with most plans, your full yearly amount is available on the first day of the plan year, even before you've paid it in.

Can you have both?

Not a regular health FSA. If you have an HSA, a general-purpose health FSA (yours or your spouse's) makes you ineligible to contribute to the HSA. A limited-purpose FSA, which only covers dental and vision, is the usual exception and can be combined with an HSA.

Why only the HSA works for the shoebox strategy

The shoebox strategy depends on two HSA features: unused money rolls over, and there's no deadline to reimburse yourself. FSA money has to be claimed within the plan year rules, so there's nothing to let grow.

Questions

Can I have an HSA and an FSA at the same time?

Not a general-purpose health FSA. A limited-purpose FSA for dental and vision can be combined with an HSA.

What happens to my HSA if I change jobs?

Nothing. The HSA is yours and the money stays in it. You may move it to another HSA provider if you like.

Does FSA money roll over?

Mostly not. Some plans allow a small carryover into the next year or a short grace period; the rest is lost.

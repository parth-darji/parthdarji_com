What happens to an HSA when you die?

By Parth Darji · Updated November 23, 2026

In shortIt depends on who the beneficiary is. A spouse can keep it as their own HSA, tax-free for medical expenses. For anyone else, the account ends and its value becomes taxable income for them, with no 20% penalty.

By beneficiary

  • Spouse: the HSA becomes the spouse's own HSA, with the same tax benefits.
  • Anyone else (children, others): the account stops being an HSA. The value is taxable income to them for the year of death. It can be reduced by the owner's qualified medical bills that they pay within one year after the death.
  • No beneficiary (estate): the value is included on the owner's final tax return.

What to do now

  • Name a beneficiary with your HSA provider, and check it after marriage, divorce or a new child.
  • If your heirs aren't your spouse, consider reimbursing yourself for your saved receipts before then, so less of the account ends up taxed.
  • Keep receipts where your family can find them.

See IRS Publication 969 and talk to an estate professional for your situation.

Questions

What happens to an HSA when the owner dies?

If the beneficiary is the spouse, it becomes their HSA. Otherwise, the account ends and its value is taxable income to the beneficiary, without the 20% penalty.

Is an inherited HSA taxable?

For a spouse, no: it stays an HSA. For anyone else, yes: the value is taxable income in the year of death, reduced by the owner's medical bills paid within a year.

How to reimburse yourself from your HSA

By Parth Darji · Updated November 23, 2026

In shortAdd up receipts you haven't been paid back for, withdraw that amount from your HSA to your bank account, and note which receipts it covers and the date. Your HSA provider reports the withdrawal on Form 1099-SA, and you report it on Form 8889 with your tax return.

Step by step

  1. Pick the receipts. Choose qualified medical expenses you paid yourself, that happened after your HSA was opened, and that no one else paid (insurance, an FSA) and you didn't deduct.
  2. Add them up. You don't need one withdrawal per receipt: one withdrawal can cover several, as long as your records show which ones.
  3. Withdraw the money. In your HSA provider's website or app, look for an option like "reimburse myself" or "pay myself", and send the amount to your bank account. If your money is invested, you may need to sell some investments to cash first.
  4. Record it. Mark each receipt as reimbursed with the withdrawal date, and keep the receipts.
  5. At tax time, your HSA provider sends Form 1099-SA showing the total you withdrew. You report it on Form 8889, including how much went to qualified medical expenses. Form 8889 in plain English.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Reimbursing the same receipt twice. Track what's already been paid back.
  • Expenses from before your HSA existed. They don't qualify.
  • Double dipping with insurance, an FSA, or a medical deduction on your return.
  • Withdrawing more than your receipts. The extra counts as a non-qualified withdrawal: taxed as income, plus a 20% penalty before age 65.

When should you reimburse yourself?

Whenever it suits you. Some people reimburse right away. Shoebox-strategy users usually wait so the money can stay invested, and take it out when they need cash or in retirement. There is no deadline under IRS Publication 969, but the longer you wait, the more important your records become.

Questions

Can I reimburse myself from my HSA for last year's medical bills?

Yes, if the expense happened after you opened your HSA, was a qualified medical expense, and wasn't paid by insurance or deducted. There is no deadline.

Do I have to match each HSA withdrawal to one receipt?

No. One withdrawal can cover several receipts. Keep a record of which receipts each withdrawal covers.

Where do HSA withdrawals go on my tax return?

Your HSA provider reports withdrawals on Form 1099-SA, and you report them on Form 8889, which you file with your Form 1040.

Lost an HSA receipt? Here's how to get proof again

By Parth Darji · Updated November 23, 2026

In shortMost medical receipts can be recreated: ask the provider for an itemized statement, print your pharmacy's prescription history, and download the EOB from your insurer. Add a card or bank statement as proof you paid. If you can't document an expense, it's safer not to reimburse yourself for it.

Where to get a replacement

  1. The provider's billing office. Doctors, dentists, labs and hospitals can usually send an itemized statement for past visits. Ask for one that shows the date of service, what was done, and what you paid.
  2. The patient portal. Many providers keep statements and payment history online for several years.
  3. Your pharmacy. Most large pharmacies can print a prescription history or a yearly summary for tax or insurance purposes. Ask at the counter or check their app.
  4. Your insurer. Explanation of Benefits (EOB) documents are usually available in your insurer's website or app. They show the service, the date and your share. How to read an EOB.
  5. Your card or bank statements. They prove you paid, though not what for, so pair them with one of the documents above.
  6. Online orders. For things like contact lenses or first-aid supplies bought online, your order history usually has an itemized invoice.

What counts as good proof

You want to be able to show who provided the care, when, what it was for, and what you paid yourself. Two documents together, such as an EOB plus a card statement, often do that when one alone doesn't. More detail: which receipts to keep.

If you can't find any proof

The IRS can ask you to show that an HSA withdrawal paid for a qualified medical expense. If you can't, the withdrawal may be taxed as income, plus a 20% additional tax if you're under 65. So if an expense can't be documented at all, the safe choice is to leave it out of your reimbursements, or at least write down everything you remember (date, provider, amount, how you paid) while you keep looking.

Stop it happening again

  • Photograph receipts the day you get them; thermal paper fades within a few years.
  • Save EOBs and statements as PDFs when they arrive.
  • Keep a backup copy somewhere other than one phone.

Questions

Can I reimburse myself from my HSA without a receipt?

You can withdraw the money, but if the IRS asks, you need records showing it paid for a qualified medical expense. Without proof, the withdrawal may be taxed plus a 20% penalty if you're under 65.

How do I get a copy of an old medical receipt?

Ask the provider's billing office for an itemized statement, check the patient portal, print your pharmacy's prescription history, or download the EOB from your insurer.

Is a credit card statement enough for an HSA receipt?

Usually not alone, because it doesn't show what you bought. Pair it with an itemized receipt, statement or EOB.

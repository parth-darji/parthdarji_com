HSA investing basics

By Parth Darji · Updated November 23, 2026

In shortMost HSAs keep your money in cash until you choose to invest part of it. Many providers require a minimum cash balance first. Invested money can grow tax-free, but it can also lose value. This page explains how it works, not what to buy.

Cash vs invested

Your HSA usually has two parts: a cash part that pays bills and the card, and an investment part, often a list of mutual funds or a brokerage window. Money moves between them when you tell it to.

Things to check with your provider

  • Minimum cash balance before you can invest. It varies by provider.
  • Fees: monthly account or investment fees, and each fund's expense ratio.
  • Investment choices: a short fund list or a full brokerage account.
  • Automatic investing of new contributions above the cash minimum.
  • Transfers: you can move your HSA to a provider with lower fees. See rollovers and transfers.

Withdrawals when money is invested

To reimburse yourself, you usually sell investments first, then move cash out. Selling inside an HSA isn't taxed. Plan ahead for big bills, because selling and transferring can take a few days.

The risks

  • Investments go up and down and can lose value, especially over short periods.
  • Money you'll need soon for medical bills is often kept in cash.
  • An HSA isn't FDIC-insured once it's invested in funds.

Why receipts matter more when you invest

If you pay bills from your own pocket and leave the HSA invested, your saved receipts let you take money out tax-free later. See the shoebox strategy and try the growth calculator.

Questions

Can I invest my HSA?

Most HSA providers let you invest part of your balance, often after a minimum cash amount. Investing is optional and comes with market risk.

Are HSA investment gains taxed?

Not federally while they stay in the HSA, and not when withdrawn for qualified medical expenses. Some states, such as California, tax HSA earnings.

HSA taxes in California and New Jersey

By Parth Darji · Updated November 23, 2026

In shortCalifornia and New Jersey don't give the state tax break on HSA contributions. You still get every federal benefit. Your state return just treats the HSA more like a regular account.

California

  • Contributions: deductible federally, but added back to your California income. Payroll HSA contributions show up as California wages.
  • Earnings: California generally taxes HSA interest, dividends and gains each year, so keep your provider's yearly statements.
  • Withdrawals for medical expenses: not taxed by California either.
  • Other withdrawals: California has its own rules for non-medical withdrawals. Check the current Schedule CA (540) and Form 3805P instructions.

New Jersey

  • Contributions: deductible federally, but taxable for New Jersey income tax. Payroll HSA contributions are added back to New Jersey wages.
  • A bill to change this (Assembly Bill 2634, 2026-2027 session) would allow a New Jersey deduction. As of November 2026 it's only been introduced, not passed.
  • Check the current New Jersey income tax instructions for how earnings and withdrawals are treated.

Is an HSA still worth it there?

Usually yes. The federal breaks are the big ones: no federal income tax on contributions, no payroll tax through work, tax-free federal growth and tax-free withdrawals for medical bills. See the triple tax advantage, and use 0% state tax in the tax savings calculator.

Other states

A few states have their own HSA rules. Check your state's tax instructions or ask a tax professional.

Questions

Are HSA contributions tax-deductible in California?

No. California doesn't follow the federal HSA deduction, so contributions are added back to California income. The federal deduction still applies.

Does New Jersey tax HSA contributions?

Yes, under current law New Jersey taxes HSA contributions. A 2026 bill (A2634) would change that but hasn't passed.

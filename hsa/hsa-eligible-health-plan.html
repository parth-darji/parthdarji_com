What is an HSA-eligible health plan?

By Parth Darji · Updated November 23, 2026

In shortTo put money into an HSA you need a qualifying high-deductible health plan (HDHP), no other disqualifying health coverage, no Medicare, and you can't be claimed as someone else's dependent. Your plan documents or insurer will usually say "HSA-eligible" or "HSA-qualified".

What makes a plan qualify (2026)

Self-onlyFamily
Minimum yearly deductible$1,700$3,400
Maximum out-of-pocket$8,500$17,000

The plan also can't pay for most care before you meet the deductible. Preventive care, such as checkups and screenings, is the main exception and can be covered from the first dollar. Source: IRS Publication 969.

Changes from 2026

A 2025 federal law expanded HSA eligibility starting in 2026: bronze and catastrophic plans bought on the health insurance marketplace are treated as HSA-compatible, telehealth before the deductible no longer disqualifies a plan, and some direct primary care arrangements are allowed. Ask your insurer or check the plan details to confirm your own plan qualifies.

Things that make you ineligible

  • Other health coverage that isn't an HDHP, such as a spouse's regular family plan that covers you.
  • A general-purpose health FSA or HRA, yours or your spouse's. A limited-purpose FSA for dental and vision is fine. HSA vs FSA.
  • Medicare, including only Part A. Your HSA after 65.
  • Being someone else's tax dependent.

Dental, vision, accident and disability insurance don't count as other coverage, so they don't affect eligibility.

Lost eligibility? Your HSA stays yours

If you switch to a plan that isn't an HDHP, you can't add new money, but everything already in the HSA stays yours. You can still invest it and use it tax-free for qualified medical expenses, including reimbursing yourself for receipts you saved.

Questions

What is the minimum deductible for an HSA-eligible plan in 2026?

$1,700 for self-only coverage and $3,400 for family coverage.

Can I keep my HSA if I change to a non-HDHP plan?

Yes. You can't add new money, but the account and everything in it stay yours, and you can still use it for qualified medical expenses.

Does a spouse's health plan affect my HSA eligibility?

It can. If their non-HDHP plan covers you, or they have a general-purpose health FSA, you generally can't contribute to an HSA.

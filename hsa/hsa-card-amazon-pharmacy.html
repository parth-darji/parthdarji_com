Using your HSA card on Amazon and at the pharmacy

By Parth Darji · Updated November 23, 2026

In shortBig retailers like Amazon, CVS, Walgreens, Walmart and Target can usually tell which items are HSA-eligible and charge only those to your HSA card. Either way, keep the itemized receipt: the IRS can ask you to prove what HSA money paid for.

Amazon

  • Eligible products are usually labeled "FSA or HSA eligible" on the product page.
  • Add your HSA card as a payment method. At checkout, Amazon can charge the eligible items to the HSA card and the rest to another card.
  • Download the invoice from your order history. It's your itemized receipt.

Pharmacies and big stores

Stores with an inventory system that knows eligible items (many large pharmacies and supermarkets) let the HSA card pay only for eligible items at the register. Ask for an itemized receipt, not just the card slip.

Why an HSA card gets declined

  • The cart has non-eligible items and the store can't split them.
  • The store isn't set up to recognize eligible items.
  • Not enough cash in the HSA, often because the money is invested.
  • The card isn't activated, or the provider flagged the purchase.

The other option: pay with your own card

Many people pay with a regular card, keep the receipt, and let the HSA money keep growing. You can reimburse yourself later, even years later, for expenses after your HSA was opened. That's the shoebox strategy, and the receipt is what makes it work.

Questions

Can I use my HSA card on Amazon?

Yes. Add it as a payment method. Amazon marks eligible items as FSA or HSA eligible and can charge only those to your HSA card.

Why was my HSA card declined?

Usually because the purchase included non-eligible items the store couldn't split, the store can't recognize eligible items, or there wasn't enough cash in the HSA.

Do I need to keep receipts if I use my HSA card?

Yes. Your HSA provider usually doesn't check, but the IRS can ask you to show that each withdrawal paid for a qualified medical expense.

HSA contribution limits

By Parth Darji · Updated November 23, 2026

In shortFor 2026 you can put up to $4,400 into an HSA with self-only coverage, or $8,750 with family coverage. If you're 55 or older, add $1,000. Employer money counts toward the limit, and you have until the tax filing deadline (usually April 15 of the next year) to contribute.

The IRS announces the next year's limits each spring. Source: IRS Publication 969.

Rules that trip people up

  • Employer contributions count. Money your employer puts in, and payroll contributions, are part of the same limit.
  • Deadline. Contributions for a tax year can usually be made until the filing deadline the following April. Tell your HSA provider which year a contribution is for.
  • Partial years. If you're only eligible for part of the year, the limit is generally prorated by month. The "last-month rule" lets you contribute the full amount if you're eligible on December 1, but you then need to stay eligible through the following year.
  • Married couples with family coverage share one family limit, split however you like. Each spouse who is 55 or older can add their own $1,000, but only into their own HSA.
  • Medicare. Contributions stop for any month you're enrolled in Medicare. Your HSA after 65.

Put in too much?

Excess contributions are charged a 6% tax for each year they stay in the account. To avoid it, ask your HSA provider to remove the excess (and what it earned) before your tax filing deadline. It's reported on Form 8889.

Questions

What is the HSA contribution limit for 2026?

$4,400 for self-only coverage and $8,750 for family coverage, plus a $1,000 catch-up if you're 55 or older.

Do employer HSA contributions count toward the limit?

Yes. Employer contributions and your own payroll contributions all count toward the same yearly limit.

When is the deadline to contribute to an HSA?

Usually the tax filing deadline of the following year, typically April 15, without extensions.

What happens if I contribute too much to my HSA?

Excess contributions face a 6% tax each year they stay in the account. Remove the excess and its earnings before the filing deadline to avoid it.

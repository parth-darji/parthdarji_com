Put too much in your HSA? Here's what to do

By Parth Darji · Updated November 23, 2026

In shortAsk your HSA provider to remove the excess contribution, plus any earnings on it, before your tax filing deadline. That avoids the 6% excise tax. The earnings are taxed as income.

Common causes

  • Your employer's contribution plus yours went over the limit. Employer money counts toward it.
  • You changed jobs and both employers' payroll put money in.
  • You enrolled in Medicare (which ends HSA eligibility) but kept contributing, or Medicare Part A started up to six months back when you claimed Social Security.
  • You switched from family to self-only coverage during the year.

Limits for each year: HSA contribution limits.

How to fix it

  1. Work out the excess for the year (Form 8889 does this).
  2. Ask your provider for a "removal of excess contribution". Most have a form. They calculate the earnings on the excess.
  3. Do it before your tax deadline, including extensions. Then there's no 6% tax.
  4. Report the earnings as other income on your tax return. Your provider sends a Form 1099-SA.

If you miss the deadline

A 6% excise tax applies (Form 5329) for every year the excess stays in the account. You can also leave it and count it toward next year's limit, but the 6% still applies for the year it was excess. See IRS Publication 969.

Questions

What is the penalty for an HSA excess contribution?

A 6% excise tax on the excess for each year it stays in the account, unless you remove it and its earnings by your tax filing deadline.

How do I remove an excess HSA contribution?

Ask your HSA provider for a removal of excess contribution before your tax deadline. They return the excess plus earnings, and the earnings are taxable income.

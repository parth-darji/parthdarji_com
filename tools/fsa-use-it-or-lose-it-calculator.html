FSA use it or lose it calculator

By Parth Darji · Updated November 23, 2026

In shortHealth FSA money you don't use by your plan's deadline is usually lost. Many plans soften this with either a carryover (up to $680 of 2026 money into 2027) or a grace period (up to 2½ extra months), never both. Check your plan, then spend the rest on things you'd buy anyway.

Money at risk

$0

Things you can usually buy with FSA money

  • Prescription glasses, sunglasses, contact lenses and solution, and an eye exam
  • Dental cleanings, fillings and other dental work
  • Over-the-counter medicine: pain relievers, allergy and cold medicine
  • Menstrual products, first aid kits, bandages, thermometers
  • Sunscreen (SPF 15+), blood pressure monitors, heating pads
  • Prescription refills and copays for appointments you've been putting off

Not sure? Search the eligibility lookup. Many plans also give a few months after the deadline (a "run-out period") to submit claims for expenses you already had, so keep the receipts.

An HSA works differently: it never expires. See HSA vs FSA.

Questions

What happens to unused FSA money?

It's usually forfeited after your plan's deadline, unless your plan allows a carryover (up to $680 of 2026 money into 2027) or a grace period of up to 2½ months.

Does my FSA roll over?

Only if your employer's plan offers a carryover, and only up to the limit. Some plans offer a grace period instead, and some offer neither. Check your plan documents.

What can I buy with leftover FSA money?

Common choices: glasses or contacts, dental work, over-the-counter medicine, first aid supplies, menstrual products and sunscreen.

Home inventory checklist for insurance

By Parth Darji · Updated November 23, 2026

In shortAfter a fire, theft or storm, you'll be asked to list what you lost. A home inventory made now, with photos, prices and receipts, makes that far easier. Do one room at a time and store a copy away from home.

For every item, record

  • What it is: name, brand and model.
  • Serial number (appliances and electronics).
  • When and where you bought it, and the price.
  • A photo, and for valuables a clear shot of the label or serial number.
  • The receipt or order email, and any warranty end date.

Room by room

  • Kitchen: appliances, cookware, small appliances, dishes, glassware.
  • Living room: TV and media gear, furniture, rugs, art, shelves of books and games.
  • Bedrooms: beds and mattresses, dressers, clothing, shoes, bags, jewelry.
  • Home office: computers, monitors, printer, phones, tablets, desk, chair.
  • Bathrooms: fixtures you own, towels, small appliances.
  • Garage and basement: tools, bikes, sports gear, lawn equipment, stored items.
  • Outdoors: patio furniture, grills, play equipment.
  • Valuables: jewelry, watches, collections, instruments, firearms, cash-value items. Many policies limit the payout for these, so ask your insurer if you need extra cover.

Photos and video

Open every closet, drawer and cabinet on camera and say what you see. A slow walk-through video of each room is quick and catches things a list misses.

Keep it safe

  • Store a copy away from the home: cloud storage, email to yourself, or a drive kept elsewhere.
  • Update it once a year and after any big purchase.
  • Keep your insurance policy number and your insurer's claims phone number with it.

Policy basics worth checking

Ask your insurer whether your policy pays actual cash value (after depreciation) or replacement cost, what the deductible is, and which items have sub-limits. This is general information, not insurance advice; the terms of your own policy decide.

Questions

What should a home inventory include?

Each item's name, brand and model, serial number, purchase date and price, a photo, and the receipt or order email, grouped by room.

Where should I keep my home inventory?

Keep a copy away from home, such as cloud storage or email, so it survives the same disaster as your belongings. Update it once a year.

Do I need receipts for an insurance claim?

Receipts help prove ownership and value, but photos, bank or card statements and order emails also help. Ask your insurer what they accept.

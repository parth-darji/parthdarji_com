Medical expense deduction calculator

By Parth Darji · Updated November 23, 2026

In shortYou can deduct medical expenses only if you itemize, and only the part above 7.5% of your adjusted gross income (AGI). It helps only when all your itemized deductions together beat the standard deduction.

Deductible medical expenses

$0

Uses the 2026 standard deduction ($16,100 single or married filing separately, $32,200 joint, $24,150 head of household, plus $2,050 or $1,650 per person 65+ or blind). Don't count expenses paid with HSA or FSA money or reimbursed by insurance. Other itemized deductions are things like state and local taxes, mortgage interest and charity. An estimate, not tax advice.

What counts as a medical expense

Doctor, dentist and hospital bills, prescriptions, glasses and contacts, insurance premiums you paid with after-tax money, and travel for medical care, under IRS Publication 502. Keep the receipts. Look up items in the eligibility lookup.

HSA or deduction?

If you have an HSA, paying medical bills from it (or reimbursing yourself later) is usually better than the deduction: it's tax-free from the first dollar, with no 7.5% floor. See the shoebox strategy.

Questions

Can I deduct medical expenses in 2026?

Yes, if you itemize: the part of your qualified medical expenses above 7.5% of your adjusted gross income. It only helps if your total itemized deductions exceed the standard deduction ($16,100 single, $32,200 joint for 2026).

Can I deduct medical bills paid with my HSA?

No. Expenses paid with HSA or FSA money are already tax-free, so they can't also be deducted.

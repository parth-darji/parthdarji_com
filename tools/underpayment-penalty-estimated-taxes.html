Underpayment penalty explained

By Parth Darji · Updated November 23, 2026

In shortIf you pay too little tax during the year, the IRS charges a penalty that works like interest on the amount you were short, for the time it was short. You can avoid it by owing under $1,000, or by paying at least the safe harbor amount on time.

How to avoid it

  • Owe under $1,000 after withholding and credits: most people avoid the penalty.
  • Pay the safe harbor: withholding plus on-time estimated payments of at least the smaller of 90% of this year's tax or 100% of last year's (110% if last year's AGI was over $150,000, or $75,000 married filing separately).

It's figured per due date

The penalty is calculated separately for each quarter. If you skip April and June and then pay a lot in September, you can still owe a penalty for the earlier quarters, even if the year's total is enough. That's why splitting your payments evenly matters. The rate is set by the IRS and changes quarterly.

Uneven income? The annualized method

If you earn most of your money late in the year (a seasonal business, a big client in the fall), you may be able to lower or remove the penalty with the annualized income installment method on Schedule AI. It matches the required payments to when you actually earned the income.

Will I get a bill?

Often the IRS works out the penalty and sends a bill, so you usually don't file Form 2210 yourself. You file it when a box in Part II applies, or when you want to use the annualized method or ask for a waiver.

Waivers

The IRS may waive the penalty if the shortfall was caused by a casualty, disaster or other unusual circumstance, or if you retired after age 62 or became disabled and the shortfall was due to reasonable cause. You ask on Form 2210.

What to do now

  1. Work out this year's tax with the 1099 tax calculator.
  2. Divide by four with the quarterly calculator.
  3. Pay each date, online if you can, and keep the confirmation.

Missed a quarter? Pay as soon as you can; the penalty grows with the time you're short. Read who needs to pay too.

Questions

How do I avoid the estimated tax underpayment penalty?

Owe less than $1,000 after withholding and credits, or pay at least the smaller of 90% of this year's tax or 100% of last year's tax (110% if last year's AGI was over $150,000), on time through the year.

Is the penalty figured per quarter?

Yes. It's figured separately for each due date, so a missed early payment can be penalized even if you caught up later.

Can the underpayment penalty be waived?

In some cases: a casualty, disaster or other unusual circumstance, or reasonable cause after retiring at 62 or later or becoming disabled. You request it on Form 2210.

Your first year with an HSA

By Parth Darji · Updated November 23, 2026

In shortYou can contribute for any month you are covered by a high deductible health plan (HDHP) on the first day of the month. Starting mid-year? The last-month rule can let you contribute the full year's amount, but only if you stay eligible through the following year.

Who can contribute

  • You are covered by an HDHP on the first day of the month.
  • You have no other health coverage, except what the IRS allows.
  • You aren't enrolled in Medicare.
  • Nobody can claim you as a dependent.

Check your plan: is my health plan HSA-eligible?

How much for 2026

Up to $4,400 for self-only coverage or $8,750 for family coverage, plus $1,000 if you are 55 or older. A mid-year start normally means a smaller limit, counted by month.

The last-month rule

If you are eligible on December 1, you are treated as eligible for the whole year and can contribute the full amount. The catch is the testing period: for 2026, you must stay eligible from December 1, 2026 through December 31, 2027. If you don't, the extra amount becomes taxable income, plus a penalty.

Save receipts from day one

The big HSA advantage is paying yourself back later, tax-free, for medical bills you paid out of pocket. That only works if you keep the receipts. See what receipts to keep and the shoebox strategy.

Questions

Can I contribute the full 2026 HSA limit if I start mid-year?

Yes, under the last-month rule if you are eligible on December 1, 2026, but you must stay eligible through December 31, 2027.

What is the 2026 HSA limit?

$4,400 self-only and $8,750 family, plus $1,000 if you are 55 or older.

Donation log builder

By Parth Darji · Updated November 23, 2026

In shortKeep a dated list of what you give. The IRS wants records: a receipt or bank record for cash, a written acknowledgment from the charity for gifts of $250 or more, and a good-condition test for clothing and household goods. This log flags what's missing and saves a PDF.

Value goods at what buyers typically pay for used items, such as thrift-shop prices, not what you paid new. The IRS says there is no fixed percentage of the original price.

Nothing you type is stored or sent. Entries stay on this page only.

Your log

General record-keeping help for the 2026 tax year, not tax advice. This log doesn't work out whether or how much you can deduct. Check the IRS pages below or ask a tax professional.

What the IRS asks you to keep

  • Cash gifts (any amount): a bank or card record, or a written record from the charity.
  • $250 or more: a written acknowledgment from the charity, which must say whether you got anything in return. Get it by the earlier of the date you file or the due date (including extensions).
  • Goods under $250: a receipt from the charity, or your own written record of the charity, date, place and what you gave.
  • Goods totalling over $500 in the year: Form 8283 goes with your return.
  • Clothing and household items: must be in good used condition or better. A single item over $500 that isn't, needs a qualified appraisal.

New for 2026: gifts if you don't itemize

If you take the standard deduction, you may be able to deduct up to $1,000 ($2,000 if married filing jointly) of cash gifts to eligible charities. Goods, donor-advised funds and supporting organizations don't count for this. If you do itemize, only the part of your gifts above 0.5% of your adjusted gross income counts. Read the IRS notice below for the details.

Questions

What records do I need for donations to charity?

For cash gifts, a bank or card record or a written record from the charity. For $250 or more, a written acknowledgment from the charity. For goods, a receipt or your own written record, and Form 8283 if your goods total more than $500 in the year.

Can I deduct donated clothes that are worn out?

Generally no. The IRS says clothing and household items must be in good used condition or better. A single item over $500 that isn't needs a qualified appraisal.

Do I have to itemize to deduct donations?

Not for cash gifts in 2026. People who take the standard deduction may deduct up to $1,000 ($2,000 joint) of cash gifts to eligible charities. Goods don't qualify for that, and itemizers can only deduct gifts above 0.5% of their adjusted gross income.

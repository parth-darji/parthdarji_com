In shortFor tax years 2025 through 2028, workers in jobs that customarily get tips can deduct qualified tips, up to $25,000 a year. It shrinks above $150,000 of income ($300,000 joint). It lowers federal income tax only; Social Security, Medicare and most state taxes still apply.

Qualified tips are voluntary cash or card tips (including shared or pooled tips) in an occupation the IRS lists as customarily tipped, reported on your W-2, 1099 or Form 4137. Automatic service charges don't count. Married couples must file jointly. Self-employed: the deduction can't exceed your net income from that work. Questions

How does no tax on tips work?

For 2025 through 2028, workers in customarily tipped jobs can deduct up to $25,000 of qualified tips a year from federal taxable income. It phases out above $150,000 of income ($300,000 joint) and doesn't remove Social Security or Medicare tax.

Can I take the tips deduction if I take the standard deduction?

Yes. It's available whether you itemize or take the standard deduction.

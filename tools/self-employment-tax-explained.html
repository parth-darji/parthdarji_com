Self-employment tax explained

By Parth Darji · Updated November 23, 2026

In shortWhen you work for yourself, you pay both halves of Social Security and Medicare: 15.3% on 92.35% of your net profit. It's on top of income tax, and half of it is deductible.

What it is

Employees pay 7.65% for Social Security and Medicare, and their employer pays another 7.65%. When you're self-employed (freelancer, contractor, gig worker, anyone paid on a 1099-NEC or 1099-K for business), you're both, so you pay the full 15.3%. It's reported on Schedule SE with your Form 1040.

How it's calculated

  1. Start with your net profit: business income minus business expenses (Schedule C).
  2. Multiply by 92.35%. This mirrors the employer half an employee never sees.
  3. Apply 12.4% Social Security up to the 2026 wage base of $184,500 (reduced by any W-2 wages you also had).
  4. Apply 2.9% Medicare on all of it, plus an extra 0.9% above $200,000 ($250,000 married filing jointly), counting wages too.

If your net earnings from self-employment are under $400, you don't owe it.

Example

$60,000 of profit × 92.35% = $55,410. × 15.3% = $8,478 of self-employment tax. Half of it, $4,239, is deducted when working out your income tax.

The half you can deduct

You can deduct half of your self-employment tax (not the extra 0.9%) from your income. It lowers income tax, not self-employment tax itself.

Lowering it legally

  • Track every business expense. Self-employment tax is on profit, so expenses reduce it directly.
  • A home office, business mileage, software and equipment all count when they're for the business.

Questions

What is the self-employment tax rate for 2026?

15.3%: 12.4% for Social Security up to the $184,500 wage base and 2.9% for Medicare, applied to 92.35% of net profit. An extra 0.9% Medicare applies above $200,000 ($250,000 joint).

Do I pay self-employment tax and income tax?

Yes. Self-employment tax is separate from income tax. Half of the self-employment tax is deductible when working out income tax.

Is there a minimum before self-employment tax applies?

Yes. If your net earnings from self-employment are under $400 for the year, you don't owe self-employment tax.

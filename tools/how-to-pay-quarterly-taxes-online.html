How to pay quarterly taxes online

By Parth Darji · Updated November 23, 2026

In shortThe free, simple way is IRS Direct Pay from your bank account: pick "Estimated Tax", form 1040-ES and the tax year. Save the confirmation number. 2026 due dates: April 15, June 15, September 15 2026, and January 15 2027.

Option 1: IRS Direct Pay (free)

  1. Go to irs.gov/payments and choose Direct Pay.
  2. Reason for payment: Estimated Tax. Apply to: 1040ES. Tax period: the year you're paying for (2026 for all four 2026 payments, including the one due January 15, 2027).
  3. Verify your identity with details from a past return.
  4. Enter the amount and your bank account, pick a date, and submit.
  5. Save the confirmation number.

Option 2: IRS Online Account

Sign in at irs.gov with your ID.me login. You can pay from your bank and see every payment you've made, which is handy when you file.

Option 3: EFTPS (free)

The Treasury's payment system. It needs an enrollment and a PIN by mail, so set it up early. Good for scheduling a whole year of payments ahead.

Option 4: Debit or credit card

Through IRS-approved card processors listed on irs.gov. They charge a processing fee, so a bank payment is usually cheaper.

Paying by mail

Mail a check with the payment voucher from Form 1040-ES. Write your SSN, the tax year and "1040-ES" on the check.

How much to pay

To avoid the underpayment penalty, pay at least the smaller of 90% of this year's tax or 100% of last year's (110% if last year's AGI was over $150,000), split over the four dates. The quarterly estimated tax calculator works it out.

State estimated taxes

Most states with an income tax have their own estimated payments on their own website. Paying the IRS doesn't cover your state.

Questions

What is the easiest way to pay estimated taxes?

IRS Direct Pay from a bank account. It's free, needs no enrollment, and gives you a confirmation number.

Which tax year do I pick for the January payment?

The previous year. The payment due January 15, 2027 is the fourth payment for tax year 2026.

Can I pay estimated taxes with a credit card?

Yes, through IRS-approved card processors, but they charge a fee. Paying from a bank account is free.

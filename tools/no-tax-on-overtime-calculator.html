No tax on overtime calculator

By Parth Darji · Updated November 23, 2026

In shortFor tax years 2025 through 2028, you can deduct the extra "half" part of overtime pay required by federal law (the premium above your regular rate), up to $12,500 a year, or $25,000 if married filing jointly. It shrinks above $150,000 of income ($300,000 joint). It lowers federal income tax only.

Estimated overtime deduction

$0

An estimate based on IRS guidance as of November 2026. Only overtime required by the Fair Labor Standards Act counts, and only the premium part: for time-and-a-half, that's the extra half. Double time still counts as the half. Overtime from union contracts or state law beyond the federal rule may not count. Social Security, Medicare and most state income taxes still apply.

How it's calculated

  1. Qualified premium = half your regular rate × overtime hours × weeks.
  2. Cap: up to $12,500 ($25,000 joint).
  3. Phase-out: the cap drops by $100 for every $1,000 of income above $150,000 ($300,000 joint).
  4. Tax saved ≈ deduction × your federal bracket.

What to keep

Keep your pay stubs and W-2 showing overtime hours and pay. Employers are expected to report qualified overtime separately; if yours doesn't, your pay stubs are your record.

Questions

How does no tax on overtime work?

For 2025 through 2028, workers can deduct the overtime premium required by the Fair Labor Standards Act, the extra half in time-and-a-half, up to $12,500 a year or $25,000 for joint filers. It reduces federal income tax, not payroll tax.

Is all overtime pay tax free?

No. Only the premium part counts: for time-and-a-half, the extra half of your rate. The regular part of overtime pay is still taxable, and Social Security and Medicare taxes still apply.

Who can't take the overtime deduction?

Married people filing separately can't. The deduction also shrinks for income above $150,000, or $300,000 for joint filers.

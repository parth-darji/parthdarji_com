Mileage deduction for freelancers

By Parth Darji · Updated November 23, 2026

In shortIf you use your own car for business, you can deduct either the IRS standard mileage rate or your actual car costs. For 2026 the business rate is 72.5 cents a mile through June 30 and 76 cents from July 1. Keep a log; it's the proof.

2026 rates

Business, Jan 1 to Jun 3072.5 cents/mile
Business, Jul 1 to Dec 3176 cents/mile
Charity14 cents/mile

The IRS raised the business rate in the middle of 2026. Use the rate for the date of each trip: a trip in March uses 72.5, a trip in October uses 76.

What counts as business miles

  • Driving to meet a client or visit a job site.
  • Trips to buy supplies, post packages, or go to the bank for the business.
  • Driving between two work locations.

What doesn't count

  • Commuting between your home and a regular workplace.
  • Personal errands, even if you made a work call on the way.

If your home is your principal place of business (see the home office deduction), trips from home to clients can count. Check the IRS guidance for your case.

Standard rate or actual expenses?

  • Standard rate: multiply business miles by the rate. Simple, and you also deduct business parking fees and tolls on top of it.
  • Actual expenses: deduct the business share of gas, repairs, insurance, registration and depreciation. More paperwork, and sometimes more money for an expensive car.
  • If you own the car, you generally must pick the standard rate in the first year you use it for business to be able to use it later. A leased car under the standard rate generally has to stay on it for the whole lease. Read the IRS rules before choosing.

Example

4,000 business miles between January and June × 72.5 cents = $2,900. 3,000 miles between July and December × 76 cents = $2,280. Deduction = $5,180, plus tolls and parking.

The log the IRS expects

For each trip: date, starting point and destination, miles, and the business reason. Also record your odometer at the start and end of the year. Write it down at the time or soon after, not months later. Photos of the odometer help.

The deduction lowers your profit, so it lowers self-employment tax as well as income tax. Try your numbers in the 1099 tax calculator. More ideas in the freelancer deductions checklist.

Questions

What is the IRS mileage rate for 2026?

For business use, 72.5 cents per mile from January 1 to June 30, 2026 and 76 cents per mile from July 1 to December 31, 2026. Charity use is 14 cents per mile.

Can I deduct my commute?

No. Driving between your home and a regular workplace is commuting and isn't deductible. Trips to clients, job sites or business errands can count.

Do I need a mileage log?

Yes. Keep the date, destination, miles and business purpose of each trip, plus your odometer readings at the start and end of the year.

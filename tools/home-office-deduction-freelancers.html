Home office deduction for freelancers

By Parth Darji · Updated November 23, 2026

In shortIf you're self-employed and use part of your home regularly and only for work, you can deduct it. The easy way: $5 per square foot, up to 300 sq ft ($1,500). Employees on a W-2 can't take it.

Who qualifies

  • Self-employed: freelancers, contractors and sole proprietors filing Schedule C.
  • Regular and exclusive use: a spare room or a clearly separate area used only for the business. A kitchen table you also eat at doesn't count.
  • Principal place of business: where you do most of the work or run the admin side, or where you meet clients.

Simplified method

Multiply the office's square feet by $5, up to 300 sq ft. A 150 sq ft office = $750. No receipts for rent or utilities needed, and nothing to recapture if you sell the home.

Regular method

Deduct the business share of real home costs: rent or mortgage interest, utilities, insurance, repairs, and depreciation if you own. The share is usually the office's area ÷ the home's area. It's more paperwork (Form 8829) and can give more in a high-cost home.

Example

A 200 sq ft office in a 1,000 sq ft apartment with $24,000 rent and $3,000 of utilities a year: regular method 20% × $27,000 = $5,400; simplified method 200 × $5 = $1,000.

Good to know

  • The deduction can't create a loss from the business under the simplified method.
  • It lowers profit, so it lowers self-employment tax as well as income tax.
  • Keep a photo and a simple floor sketch of the office with your records.

Questions

How much is the simplified home office deduction?

$5 per square foot of the office, up to 300 square feet, so at most $1,500 a year.

Can W-2 employees take the home office deduction?

No. Under current federal law, employees can't deduct a home office. It's for self-employed people.

Does the office have to be a separate room?

No, but the space must be used regularly and only for your business. A shared space like a dining table doesn't qualify.

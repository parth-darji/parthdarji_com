Freelancer tax deductions checklist

By Parth Darji · Updated November 23, 2026

In shortBusiness expenses that are ordinary and necessary for your work lower your profit, and that lowers both income tax and self-employment tax. For anything used personally too, deduct only the business share. Keep the receipt.

Where you work

  • Home office: a space used regularly and only for the business. How it works.
  • Coworking or office rent.

Tools of the trade

  • Software and subscriptions you use for clients: design, code, accounting, cloud storage.
  • Computer, phone and equipment: often deductible in the year you buy them, or depreciated over several years.
  • Phone and internet: the business-use share.

Getting around

  • Car use for business: the IRS standard mileage rate or actual costs, business miles only. Commuting to a regular workplace doesn't count. Keep a mileage log.
  • Business travel: transport and lodging away from home; business meals are generally 50% deductible.

Running the business

  • Payment app, bank and card processing fees.
  • Advertising, your website and domain.
  • Accountant, lawyer and other professional fees.
  • Courses that keep up or improve skills for the work you do now.
  • Business insurance and licenses.

Personal deductions tied to self-employment

  • Half of your self-employment tax.
  • Self-employed health insurance premiums, if you weren't eligible for an employer plan.
  • Retirement contributions to a SEP-IRA or solo 401(k).

Records to keep

Receipts or invoices, bank and card statements, a mileage log, and a note of the business purpose for anything unclear. Scan paper receipts; ink fades.

Questions

What can freelancers deduct?

Ordinary and necessary business expenses: home office, business share of phone and internet, software, equipment, business car use, travel, fees, advertising and professional services, plus half of self-employment tax, self-employed health insurance and retirement contributions.

Can I deduct my phone bill as a freelancer?

The business-use share, yes. If you use the phone 40% for work, you can deduct about 40% of the bill.

Do deductions lower self-employment tax?

Business expenses on Schedule C do, because self-employment tax is figured on profit. Personal deductions like the standard deduction don't.

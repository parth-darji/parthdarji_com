How to value donated clothes and household items

By Parth Darji · Updated November 23, 2026

In shortDonated goods are worth their fair market value: roughly what a buyer would pay for them used, in a thrift or consignment shop. That's usually far less than you paid. Clothing and household items must be in good used condition or better. The records you need grow at $250, $500 and $5,000.

What "fair market value" means

Fair market value is the price a willing buyer and a willing seller would agree on. For used clothes and household goods, the IRS says there is no fixed formula: the prices buyers actually pay in thrift and consignment shops are an indication of value. What you originally paid doesn't set the value.

Condition matters

  • Clothing and household items (furniture, electronics, appliances, linens and similar) must be in good used condition or better to be deductible.
  • An item not in good used condition can count only if you claim more than $500 for it and attach a qualified appraisal and Form 8283.
  • Art, antiques, jewelry and collections follow other rules; see Publication 561.

Where to find typical values

Many charities publish their own value guides with price ranges for common items, for example the Salvation Army donation value guide. They are a starting point, not an IRS table: you decide the value based on the actual item and its condition, and you should note how you got the number.

Records you need, by amount

Under $250A receipt from the charity (name, date, place, description). At an unattended drop box, your own written record, including the condition and how you set the value.
$250 to $500A written acknowledgment from the charity, received by the time you file.
Over $500Also Form 8283, Section A, filed with your return.
Over $5,000Form 8283, Section B, and generally a qualified appraisal.

For items you deduct as a group (all the clothes in one drop-off), the amounts apply to the total. Check Publication 526 for how similar items are combined.

A simple routine

  1. Before the drop-off, list each item, its condition and the value you picked.
  2. Take a photo of the items.
  3. Ask for a receipt, and keep it with your list.
  4. At year end, add the totals and check which records are still missing.

Questions

How much are donated clothes worth for taxes?

Their fair market value: about what buyers pay for similar used items in thrift or consignment shops. That's usually much less than the original price. Charities' value guides give typical ranges.

Can I deduct clothes in poor condition?

Generally no. Clothing and household items must be in good used condition or better, unless you claim more than $500 for a single item and attach a qualified appraisal and Form 8283.

When do I need Form 8283?

When your total deduction for donated goods is over $500. Section B and generally a qualified appraisal are needed for items or groups of similar items over $5,000.

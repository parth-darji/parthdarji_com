Form 8283 explained

By Parth Darji · Updated November 23, 2026

In shortFile Form 8283 with your return when your deduction for donated items (not money) is more than $500. Use Section A for items or groups worth $5,000 or less, and Section B for anything over $5,000, which also needs a qualified appraisal.

Who needs it

You need Form 8283 if your deduction for a noncash gift is more than $500, or if a group of similar items adds up to more than $500. Clothes, furniture, electronics, cars, stock and art are all noncash gifts. Cash gifts never go on this form.

Section A or Section B?

Section AItems or groups of similar items worth $5,000 or less. Also publicly traded securities, even above $5,000.
Section BItems or groups worth more than $5,000. Needs a separate qualified appraisal, plus signatures from the appraiser and the charity.

"Similar items" means things of the same kind, like all the clothing or all the books you gave, even to different charities.

What you'll fill in (Section A)

  • The charity's name and address.
  • A description of the items and their condition.
  • The date you gave them, when and how you got them, and what you paid (for some items).
  • Their fair market value and the method you used to find it.

The appraisal is separate

Form 8283 is a summary, not an appraisal. For Section B items you need a real appraisal from a qualified appraiser, and you keep it with your records.

Records to keep

  • Receipts or acknowledgments from each charity (see donation receipt requirements).
  • Your list of items, their condition and how you valued them (see how to value donated items).
  • Photos of the items before you gave them.

Sources

  • IRS: Instructions for Form 8283
  • IRS: About Form 8283
  • IRS Publication 561: Determining the value of donated property

Questions

When do I need to file Form 8283?

When your deduction for donated property is more than $500, for one item or a group of similar items. Money gifts are not reported on it.

What is the difference between Section A and Section B?

Section A is for items or groups worth $5,000 or less (and publicly traded securities). Section B is for items or groups over $5,000 and requires a qualified appraisal.

Is Form 8283 an appraisal?

No. It's a summary that goes with your return. Section B items need a separate qualified appraisal that you keep.

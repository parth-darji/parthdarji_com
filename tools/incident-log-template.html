In shortWhen something goes wrong (a landlord that won't fix things, a problem at work, a car accident, a neighbor dispute), notes written at the time are far more useful than memories later. Add dated entries here and save them as a PDF or text file.

Your log

General record-keeping help, not legal advice. Rules about what can be used in a dispute differ by place and situation. For legal questions, ask a lawyer or a legal aid group (the Legal Services Corporation explains how to find free legal aid).

How to keep a useful log

  1. Write it soon. Add the entry the same day, while you remember clearly.
  2. Stick to facts. What you saw, heard and did. Save opinions and guesses for separate notes.
  3. Include the basics. Date, time, place, who was there, and what was said, using exact words if you can.
  4. Keep the originals. Don't edit photos, messages or emails. Save copies somewhere safe and note them in the entry.
  5. Don't change old entries. If you remember more later, add a new entry with today's date.
  6. Keep it private and backed up. Store it where only you can see it, with a second copy elsewhere.

When people keep one

  • Landlord or tenant problems: repairs asked for, answers received, dates of visits.
  • Problems at work: what happened, who was there, what you reported and to whom.
  • A car accident or injury: what you saw, photos, who you spoke to, dates of treatment.
  • Ongoing disputes with a neighbor, a contractor or a company.
  • Parenting-time exchanges and expenses.

Questions

What should an incident log include?

The date and time, where it happened, what happened in plain facts, who was involved or saw it, what evidence you have (photos, messages, documents), and what you did or said afterward.

Is my information stored when I use this builder?

No. Entries stay on the page in your browser and are never sent anywhere. Print, save as PDF or download the text file before you close the page, because the entries are lost when you leave.

Is an incident log legal advice or proof?

No. It's a personal record to help you stay organized. What can be used in a dispute differs by place and situation, so ask a lawyer or legal aid group about your case.

Do I need to pay estimated taxes?

By Parth Darji · Updated November 23, 2026

In shortYou generally need to pay estimated taxes if you expect to owe $1,000 or more when you file, after your withholding and credits. Freelancers and 1099 workers usually do, because no tax is taken out of their payments.

Who usually needs to

  • Freelancers, contractors and gig workers paid on a 1099.
  • Sole proprietors and partners.
  • Anyone with large income that has no tax withheld: interest, dividends, rent, capital gains, or a big side income on top of a job.

The $1,000 rule

Add up your expected tax for the year (income tax plus self-employment tax), subtract withholding and credits. If you'll still owe $1,000 or more, you generally should pay in during the year.

How much is enough (safe harbor)

You avoid the penalty if your withholding plus on-time payments add up to at least the smaller of:

  • 90% of this year's tax, or
  • 100% of last year's tax (110% if last year's adjusted gross income was over $150,000, or $75,000 if married filing separately).

Last year's number is easier because it's already known. The quarterly estimated tax calculator works out each payment.

When withholding is enough

If you also have a job, you can raise your W-4 withholding to cover the side income instead of making estimated payments. The IRS Tax Withholding Estimator helps you choose the amount.

When you don't need to

Generally not required if you had no tax liability last year (and you were a US citizen or resident for the whole year and last year covered 12 months). Check the IRS rules for your case.

The dates

For 2026: April 15, June 15, September 15, 2026 and January 15, 2027. See how to pay online.

Questions

When do I have to pay estimated taxes?

Generally when you expect to owe $1,000 or more after subtracting withholding and credits. Freelancers and 1099 workers usually do, since nothing is taken out of their payments.

Can my job's withholding cover my side income?

Yes. You can raise the withholding on your W-4 so the extra tax is taken from your paycheck, which can replace estimated payments.

Do I need to pay if I owed nothing last year?

Generally no, if you had no tax liability last year, were a US citizen or resident for the whole year, and last year covered 12 months. Check the IRS rules for your case.
